Choosing a Sample

MethodHow it worksExample: 60 of 1,200 students
Simple random sample (SRS)Every group of the same size has the same chance of being chosen.A random number generator picks 60 student numbers.
StratifiedSplit the population into groups that are alike (strata). Take a random sample from each group.Randomly pick 15 students from each grade.
ClusterSplit the population into groups (clusters). Randomly pick some whole groups.Randomly pick 3 homerooms. Ask everyone in them.
SystematicPick a random start. Then take every kkth person.Start at a random student from 1 to 20. Take every 20th student.
Convenience (bad)Take whoever is easy to reach. No chance is used.Ask the first 60 students who walk in the door.

Stratified: a few from every group.   Cluster: everyone from a few groups.

Experiment Designs

DesignHow it worksExample
Completely randomizedChance alone puts each subject in a treatment group.40 volunteers: chance picks 20 for the new pill and 20 for the placebo.
Randomized blockSplit the subjects into groups that are alike (blocks). Then chance assigns the treatments inside each block.40 gym members. The blocks are beginners and experienced members. Chance picks who gets each workout plan inside each block.
Matched pairsEach subject gets both treatments, in a random order. Or pair up very similar subjects, and chance picks who gets which.Each runner runs once in each shoe. A coin flip decides which shoe goes first.

A good experiment has: a control group to compare to, random assignment to make the groups alike, and replication (many subjects).

Blocks and strata: both split people into groups that are alike, then use chance inside each group. In a stratified sample, chance picks who is in the sample. In a randomized block design, chance picks which treatment each subject gets.
