Unit Review - Unit 5: Study Design - DHS Statistics
NameDatePeriod

One study, every tool. A school has 800 students. A company says its new homework app raises quiz scores. The principal wants to find out if that is true.

5.1 Who is in the study?

The population is the whole group. The sample is the part we collect data from.

 Population: all 800 students at the school.
Idea 1: Post a link: “Did the app help you?” 150 students answer. This is voluntary response bias. Students with strong feelings answer most.
Idea 2: Compare students who chose the app with students who did not. This is an observational study. Students who chose the app may already work harder.

5.2 Choose the sample.

Use chance.
Stratified: a few from every group.
Cluster: everyone from a few groups.

 Randomly pick 10 students from each grade, 9 to 12. The sample is 40 students.
This is a stratified sample. Every grade is in it, and chance picks the students.

5.3 Run an experiment.

Random assignment makes the groups alike. The control group does not get the new treatment.

 Explanatory variable: app or paper.   Response variable: quiz score.
Group 2 is the control group. Both groups get the same homework and take the same quiz.

5.4 What can it prove?

Random sample: we can generalize.
Random assignment: we can say cause.
Less than 5% by chance: statistically significant.

 App group: x‾=82\bar{x} = 82.  Paper group: x‾=74\bar{x} = 74.  Difference: 82−74=882 - 74 = 8 points.
Shuffle the 40 scores into two random groups of 20. Repeat 100 times. Only 2 of the 100 shuffles gave 8 or more (2%). The result is statistically significant.
Random sample: we can generalize to all 800 students.
Random assignment: the app caused the higher scores.

A bigger sample does not fix bias. If 1,000 people choose to answer a poll, it is still a voluntary response sample. Only chance makes a sample represent the population.
