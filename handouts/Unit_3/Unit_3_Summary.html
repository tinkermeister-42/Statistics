Parts of a Two-Way Table

60 students answered two questions: Do you play a sport? Do you have a job? A two-way table shows the answers to both questions at once. Every example on this sheet uses this table.

JobNo jobTotal
Sport82230

PartWhat it isExample
CategoriesThe answers to each question. They name the rows and the columns.Rows: Sport, No sport. Columns: Job, No job.
CellCounts students who match both labels.8 students play a sport and have a job.
TotalsIn the margins: the Total row and the Total column.30 students play a sport. 20 have a job.
Grand totalThe corner.60 students in all.
CheckEach row adds to its total. Each column adds to its total.8+22=308 + 22 = 30.  8+12=208 + 12 = 20.

KindWhat it isExample
Relative frequency tableDivide every count by the grand total. This gives the percent of all students.860≈13%\dfrac{8}{60} \approx 13\%. About 13% of all the students play a sport and have a job. The four inside cells add to 100%.
Marginal distributionPercents from the totals (the margins). Divide by the grand total.Job: 2060≈33%\dfrac{20}{60} \approx 33\%.  No job: 4060≈67%\dfrac{40}{60} \approx 67\%.
Conditional distributionPercents inside one group. Divide by that group's total.Students who play a sport.
Job: 830≈27%\dfrac{8}{30} \approx 27\%.  No job: 2230≈73%\dfrac{22}{30} \approx 73\%.

Check the denominator: “Of the students who play a sport, what percent have a job?” Divide by the sport total (30). Do not divide by 60. Do not divide by the job total (20).

Association

StepWhat to doExample
1. FindFind the conditional distribution of each group.Sport: 830≈27%\dfrac{8}{30} \approx 27\% have a job.
No sport: 1230=40%\dfrac{12}{30} = 40\% have a job.
2. CompareCompare the conditional distributions of the two groups. A segmented bar chart shows them side by side.27% and 40% are different.
3. DecideConditional percents are different in the groups: the variables are associated. Conditional percents are about the same: there is no association.Playing a sport and having a job are associated. In this group, students who play a sport are less likely to have a job.

Cause: an association only says the two things happen together. It does not prove cause.
