\sumhead{Venn Diagram and Two-Way Table}
\begin{tabular}{|L{1.45in}|L{2.5in}|L{2.52in}|}\hline
\textbf{Venn diagram} & \textbf{Two-way table} & \textbf{Example}\\ \hline
\textbf{Overlap}: both groups & The cell for Sport \textbf{and} Job. & 8 play a sport \textbf{and} have a job.\\ \hline
\textbf{One circle only} & The cell for Sport and No job. The cell for No sport and Job. & Sport only: $30 - 8 = 22$. Job only: $20 - 8 = 12$.\\ \hline
\textbf{Outside} the circles: neither group & The cell for No sport and No job. & $22 + 8 + 12 = 42$ are in at least one circle. Neither: $60 - 42 = 18$.\\ \hline
A \textbf{whole circle} & That group's total. & The ``Plays a sport'' circle holds $22 + 8 = 30$ students, not 22.\\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\sumhead{Percents from a Two-Way Table}
\begin{center}\sportjob\end{center}

{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{2.3}%
\begin{tabular}{|L{1.45in}|L{2.5in}|L{2.52in}|}\hline
\textbf{Kind} & \textbf{What it is} & \textbf{Example}\\ \hline
Relative frequency table & Divide \textbf{every} count by the \textbf{grand total}. This gives the percent of \textbf{all} students. & $\dfrac{8}{60} \approx 13\%$. About 13\% of \textbf{all} the students play a sport and have a job. The four inside cells add to 100\%.\\ \hline
Marginal distribution & Percents from the \textbf{totals} (the margins). Divide by the \textbf{grand total}. & Job: $\dfrac{20}{60} \approx 33\%$. \ No job: $\dfrac{40}{60} \approx 67\%$.\\ \hline
Conditional distribution & Percents inside \textbf{one group}. Divide by that group's \textbf{total}. & Students who play a sport.\newline Job: $\dfrac{8}{30} \approx 27\%$. \ No job: $\dfrac{22}{30} \approx 73\%$.\\ \hline
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{Check the denominator:} ``Of the students who \textbf{play a sport}, what percent have a job?'' Divide by the sport total (30). Do not divide by 60. Do not divide by the job total (20).

\sumhead{Association}
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{2.3}%
\begin{tabular}{|L{1.45in}|L{2.5in}|L{2.52in}|}\hline
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{What to do} & \textbf{Example}\\ \hline
1. Find & Find the conditional distribution of each group. & Sport: $\dfrac{8}{30} \approx 27\%$ have a job.\newline No sport: $\dfrac{12}{30} = 40\%$ have a job.\\ \hline
2. Compare & Compare the conditional distributions of the two groups. A segmented bar chart shows them side by side. & 27\% and 40\% are \textbf{different}.\\ \hline
3. Decide & Conditional percents are \textbf{different} in the groups: the variables are \textbf{associated}. Conditional percents are about the \textbf{same}: there is \textbf{no association}. & Playing a sport and having a job are \textbf{associated}. In this group, students who play a sport are less likely to have a job.\\ \hline
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{Cause:} an association only says the two things happen together. It does not prove cause.
