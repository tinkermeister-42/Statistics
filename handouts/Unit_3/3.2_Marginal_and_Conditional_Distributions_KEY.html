3.2  Marginal and Conditional Distributions

Mini Lesson - Unit 3: Two-Way Tables - DHS Statistics
Teacher Key: answers in red

Do students who play a sport have jobs as often as students who do not? To answer, compare percents inside each group.

JobNo jobTotal
Sport82230
No sport121830
Total204060
What it isExample
Marginal distributionPercents from the totals (the margins). Divide by the grand total. Job: 2060≈33%\dfrac{20}{60} \approx 33\%    No job: 4060≈67%\dfrac{40}{60} \approx 67\%
Conditional distributionPercents inside one group. Divide by that group's total. Students who play a sport:
Job: 830≈27%\dfrac{8}{30} \approx 27\%    No job: 2230≈73%\dfrac{22}{30} \approx 73\%

Compare the conditional distributions.

Sport: 830≈27%\dfrac{8}{30} \approx 27\% have a job.

No sport: 1230=40%\dfrac{12}{30} = 40\% have a job.

The percents are different. Playing a sport and having a job are associated. In this group, students who play a sport are less likely to have a job.

If the percents were about the same, there would be no association.

Gotcha: check the denominator.
“Of the students who play a sport, what percent have a job?” Divide by the sport total (30), not by 60 and not by the job total (20).
3.2  Marginal and Conditional Distributions - Practice

Work with a partner. Compare answers when you finish each problem.

1. Use the table of how 80 students get to school.
BusCarWalkTotal
9th grade18121040
10th grade1420640
Total32321680

Find the marginal distribution of how students get to school.

Bus: 3280=40%\frac{32}{80} = 40\%.  Car: 3280=40%\frac{32}{80} = 40\%.  Walk: 1680=20%\frac{16}{80} = 20\%.
2. Use the same table.
  1. Find the conditional distribution of how 9th graders get to school.
    Bus 1840=45%\frac{18}{40} = 45\%,  Car 1240=30%\frac{12}{40} = 30\%,  Walk 1040=25%\frac{10}{40} = 25\%
  2. Find the conditional distribution of how 10th graders get to school.
    Bus 1440=35%\frac{14}{40} = 35\%,  Car 2040=50%\frac{20}{40} = 50\%,  Walk 640=15%\frac{6}{40} = 15\%
  3. Is grade associated with how students get to school? Explain.
    Yes. The percents are different. For example, 50% of 10th graders come by car, but only 30% of 9th graders.
3. Use the same table.
  1. What percent of 10th graders come by car?  2040=50%\frac{20}{40} = 50\%
  2. What percent of car riders are 10th graders?  2032=62.5%\frac{20}{32} = 62.5\%
  3. Why are the answers to (a) and (b) different?
    They divide by different groups: (a) by the 40 10th graders, (b) by the 32 car riders.
4. Is each statement about the school table a marginal or a conditional percent?
  1. 40% of all students ride the bus.  Marginal
  2. Of the students who walk, 62.5% are 9th graders.  Conditional (only walkers)
  3. Half of the students are 10th graders.  Marginal
  4. 25% of 9th graders walk.  Conditional (only 9th graders)
