Standard Deviation

Standard deviationThe SD is about how far the values usually are from the mean.
Small SDThe values are close to the mean. Player A scores close to 10 every game.
Large SDThe values are spread out. Player B might score 2 or 18.
FactsThe SD is never negative. It is 0 only when every value is the same. It has the same units as the data (points, minutes, inches).
StepExample: 7, 7, 10, 13, 13
1. Find the mean, $\bar{x}$.$\bar{x} = 50 \div 5 = 10$
2. Subtract the mean from each value.$-3, -3, 0, 3, 3$
3. Square each result.$9, 9, 0, 9, 9$
4. Add the squares.$\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2 = 9 + 9 + 0 + 9 + 9 = 36$
5. Divide by $n - 1$. This is the variance.$36 \div 4 = 9$
6. Take the square root.$s = \sqrt{9} = 3$
The formula:  $s = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}$ $\Sigma$ (capital sigma) means add up. $\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2$ means: find $(x - \bar{x})^2$ for every value, then add them all. $n$ is how many values there are. $s$ is the standard deviation.

Choosing Measures

Shape of the dataCenterSpread
Symmetric, no outliersmeanstandard deviation
Skewed, or has outliersmedianIQR

Why: an outlier pulls the mean and the SD. It does not pull the median and the IQR much.

Comparing Two Distributions

AskExample: hours of sleep last night
ShapeSymmetric or skewed?9th grade is symmetric. 12th grade is skewed right.
CenterWhich group is higher? (median or mean)The median for 9th grade (8 hours) is greater than for 12th grade (6 hours). 9th graders usually sleep about 2 hours more.
SpreadWhich group is more spread out? (IQR or SD)Both groups have an IQR of 2 hours. The spreads are about the same.
OutliersAny values far from the rest?One 12th grader slept 12 hours. That is an outlier (the upper fence is 10).

Use comparing words: “The median for 9th grade is 2 hours greater than for 12th grade.” Use numbers and the context.

Percentiles

PercentileExample: 20 students took a test
The percent of the data that is below the value.
$\dfrac{\text{number of values below}}{\text{total number of values}} \times 100$		 15 students scored below 85. $15 \div 20 = 0.75$. A score of 85 is at the 75th percentile. It is higher than 75% of the class.
A percentile is not a percent score. Scoring at the 75th percentile does not mean you got 75% of the questions right. It means you scored higher than 75% of the people.

Z-Scores

What it tellsExample: mean 70, SD 10
Z-score
$z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$		 How many SDs a value is above or below the mean. $\mu$ (mu) is the mean. $\sigma$ (small sigma) is the SD. $z = \dfrac{85 - 70}{10} = 1.5$
85 is 1.5 SDs above the mean.
Sign of $z$$z > 0$: above the mean.
$z < 0$: below the mean.  $z = 0$: equal to the mean.		 55 has $z = -1.5$. It is 1.5 SDs below the mean.
Going backward$x = \mu + z\sigma$$z = -2$:  $70 + (-2)(10) = 50$
ComparingZ-scores let you compare values from different data sets. Ana: 85, $z = 1.5$. Ben: 80 (mean 74, SD 3), $z = 2$. Ben did better compared to his class.

Normal Distributions and the Empirical Rule

{{fig Unit_2/2.4_empirical w=3in left}}
WithinFromAbout
1 SD$\mu - \sigma$ to $\mu + \sigma$68%
2 SDs$\mu - 2\sigma$ to $\mu + 2\sigma$95%

A normal distribution is bell-shaped and symmetric. The mean and the median are in the center (they are equal). Two numbers describe it: the mean $\mu$ (center) and the SD $\sigma$ (spread).

Example: heights, mean 64 inches, SD 3 inchesAnswer
Percent from 61 to 67 inches68% (within 1 SD)
Percent from 58 to 70 inches95% (within 2 SDs)
Percent taller than 67 inches$13.5\% + 2.35\% + 0.15\% = 16\%$
Percentile of 67 inches$100\% - 16\% = 84\%$, the 84th percentile

Changes: a larger SD makes the curve wider and flatter. A different mean moves the curve left or right.

Normal Calculations with a Z-Table

The z-table gives the area to the left of $z$. That is the proportion of the data below $z$.

StepExample: heights
1. Draw the curve. Shade the part the question asks about.Shorter than 68 inches: shade below 68.
2. Find each z-score:  $z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$. Round to 2 decimal places.$z = \frac{68 - 64}{3} = 1.33$
3. Look up each z-score in the table.Row 1.3, column .03: .9082, so 90.82%
4. Below: use the table. Above: $1 -$ table.
Between: subtract the two table values.		 Taller than 68: $1 - .9082 = .0918$, 9.18%
62 to 68: $z = \frac{62 - 64}{3} = -0.67$, table .2514.
$.9082 - .2514 = .6568$, 65.68%

Normal Calculations in Reverse

StepExample: heights, 90th percentile
1. Draw the curve. Shade the area to the left.90% is to the left. The area is .9000.
2. Look inside the table for the area closest to it.The closest area is .8997.
3. Read the z-score from the row and the column.Row 1.2, column .08:  $z = 1.28$
4. Find the value:  $x = \mu + z\sigma$$64 + 1.28 \times 3 = 67.84$, about 67.8 inches
