The empirical rule only works for whole SDs (1, 2, or 3). A z-table works for any z-score.

The z-table gives the area to the left of $z$. That is the proportion of the data below $z$.

Reading the table: Find $z = 1.33$. Go down to the row 1.3. Go across to the column .03. The area is .9082.

{{fig Unit_2/2.5_three_areas}}

Steps

1.Draw the curve. Shade the part the question asks about.
2.Find each z-score:  $z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$. Round to 2 decimal places.
3.Look up each z-score in the table.
4.Below: use the table.  Above: $1 -$ table.  Between: subtract.

Example: Heights of adult women: mean 64 inches, SD 3 inches.

{{fig Unit_2/2.5_heights_between}}
Shorter than 68 inches$z = \dfrac{68 - 64}{3} = 1.33$   table: .9082   90.82%
Taller than 68 inches$1 - .9082 = .0918$   9.18%
From 62 to 68 inches$z = \dfrac{62 - 64}{3} = -0.67$   table: .2514   $.9082 - .2514 = .6568$   65.68%
