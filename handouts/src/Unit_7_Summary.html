ToolHow it worksExample: goals in a game
Expected value$E(X) = \Sigma\, x \cdot P(x)$.  $E(X)$ means the expected value of $X$. Multiply each value by its probability. Then add them all up.$E(X) = 0 + 0.3 + 0.6 + 0.6 + 0.4$
$= 1.9$ goals
Mean of $X$The expected value is also called the mean of $X$, written $\mu_X$ ($\mu$ is “mu”).$\mu_X = 1.9$ goals
What it meansA long-run average. It tells you what happens over many tries. It does not tell you what happens on one try.Over many games, the team scores about 1.9 goals per game on average. No single game has 1.9 goals.

Fair or Not?

What it meansExample
Fair method of choosingEvery person has the same probability of being chosen.Roll a number cube. 1 or 2: Ana, 3 or 4: Ben, 5 or 6: Cai. Each has $\frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$. Fair.
Not a fair methodNot every person has the same probability.Flip 2 coins. HH: Ana, TT: Ben, one of each: Cai. Ana $\frac{1}{4}$, Ben $\frac{1}{4}$, Cai $\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. Not fair.
Fair gameThe expected value of the money you win or lose is 0. On average, no one wins or loses money.Roll a number cube. You win \$3 if you roll a 6. You pay \$1 for any other number.
$E(X) = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{6} + (-1) \cdot \frac{5}{6} = -\frac{2}{6} \approx -\$0.33$
You lose about 33 cents a game. Not fair.

Expected Payoff

A lottery ticket costs \$2. 1 in 1,000 tickets wins \$500. 1 in 50 tickets wins \$10. The rest win nothing.

OutcomeNet gain $x$$P(x)$$x \cdot P(x)$

Win nothing$-2$0.979$-1.958$
$E(X) = \Sigma\, x \cdot P(x)$$-1.30$
ToolHow it worksExample: the lottery ticket
Net gainNet gain $=$ what you win $-$ what you paid. It is negative when you lose money.Win \$500: $500 - 2 = 498$.
Win nothing: $-2$.
Expected payoffLet $X$ = the net gain. Then $E(X) = \Sigma\, x \cdot P(x)$.$0.498 + 0.16 + (-1.958) = -1.30$
What it meansThe amount you win or lose on average.On average, a buyer loses \$1.30 per ticket. The lottery keeps that money.

Negative expected payoff: you lose money on average.    Expected payoff of 0: the game is fair.

Protection Plans

StepWhat to doExample: a phone
Expected cost with no planMultiply the chance it breaks by the cost to replace it.A phone costs \$300 to replace. The chance it breaks this year is 0.10.
$0.10 \cdot 300 = \$30$
CompareCompare it to the cost of the plan.The plan costs \$40 for the year. On average, the plan costs \$10 more. That is how the company makes money.
Think about riskExpected value is one part of a decision. Risk is another part: could you pay the big bill if it came?The plan protects you from a sudden \$300 bill. If you could not pay it, the plan might be worth it to you.
