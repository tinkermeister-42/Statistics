A school has 1,200 students. The principal wants to ask 60 of them about the new schedule. How should she choose the 60? The best methods use chance to pick, so no group is favored.

MethodHow it worksExample (60 of 1,200)
Simple random sample (SRS)Every group of the same size has the same chance of being chosen. Like drawing names from a hat.Number the students 1 to 1,200. A random number generator picks 60 numbers.
StratifiedSplit the population into groups (strata). People in the same group are alike in some way. Take a random sample from each group.Randomly pick 15 students from each grade: 9th, 10th, 11th, 12th.
ClusterSplit the population into groups (clusters). Randomly pick some whole groups.Randomly pick 3 homerooms. Ask everyone in them.
SystematicPick a random start. Then take every $k$th person. $k$ is the population size divided by the sample size.Start at a random student between 1 and 20. Then take every 20th student on the list.
Convenience (bad)Take whoever is easy to reach.Ask the first 60 students who walk in the door.

Stratified or cluster?  Stratified: a few from every group.  Cluster: everyone from a few groups.

Using a random digit table. Each digit is random. Read the digits in pairs for 2-digit numbers.

Why random?
Chance does not pick favorites. A random sample is not biased toward any group.

3. A school wants to survey 100 of its 1,000 students about sports. About half of the students play a sport.
  1. Describe how to take a stratified sample.
    {{a}}Split the students into athletes and non-athletes. Randomly choose 50 from each group.{{/a}}
  2. Why might a stratified sample be better than an SRS here?
    {{a}}It makes sure both groups are in the sample in the right numbers. An SRS could, by chance, pick too many athletes.{{/a}}
4. A farmer has 50 rows of corn. She wants to check 500 plants for insects. She randomly picks 5 rows and checks every plant in them.
  1. What method is this?  {{a}}Cluster{{/a}}
  2. Why would a farmer choose this method?
    {{a}}It is quicker. She only walks 5 rows instead of searching the whole field.{{/a}}
5. Maya wants to know how students feel about school lunch. She surveys the first 40 students in the lunch line.
  1. What method is this? What is the problem?
    {{a}}Convenience. Students who skip lunch or come late are left out, so the results are biased.{{/a}}
  2. Describe a better way for Maya to choose 40 students.
    {{a}}Get a list of all students. Use a random number generator to pick 40 names (an SRS).{{/a}}
