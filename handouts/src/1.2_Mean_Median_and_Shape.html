Mean (also called the average) Median (the middle)
1. Add all the values.
2. Divide by how many values there are.		 1. Put the values in order.
2. Find the middle value.
Data:  2,  3,  8,  3,  4
$2 + 3 + 8 + 3 + 4 = 20$      $20 \div 5 = 4$
Mean = 4
In symbols:  $\bar{x} = \dfrac{\Sigma x}{n} = \dfrac{20}{5} = 4$		 In order:  2,  3,  3,  4,  8      Median = 3
Even number of values? Two numbers are in the middle. Find the number halfway between them.
2,  3,  5,  8      $(3 + 5) \div 2 = 4$    Median = 4

$\Sigma$ is the Greek capital letter sigma. It means add up.  $\Sigma x$ means add up all the values.  $\bar{x}$ (“x-bar”) is the mean.  $n$ is how many values.

Shape changes the mean. The tail is the long, low side of a graph. The mean is pulled toward the tail. The median is not pulled much.

{{fig Unit_1/1.2_shapes}}

Real example: US household income (2024). Most households earn a medium amount. A small number of households earn millions of dollars. That makes a long tail on the right.

{{fig Unit_1/1.2_income}}

The median is a better description of a typical household. Most households earn less than the mean.

Source: US Census Bureau, Income in the United States: 2024. Median \$83,730. Mean about \$121,000.

2. Six students timed their walk to the bus stop, in minutes.

15,  22,  18,  30,  19,  16

  1. Find the mean. {{a}}$120 \div 6 = 20$ minutes{{/a}}
  2. Find the median. {{a}}15, 16, 18, 19, 22, 30  $\rightarrow$  $(18 + 19) \div 2 = 18.5$ minutes. The 30 pulls the mean above the median.{{/a}}

7. Guess the shape of each data set: skewed left, symmetric, or skewed right. Explain.
  1. The price of every house sold in Columbia this year.
    {{a}}Skewed right. Most houses are medium price. A few cost much more.{{/a}}
  2. Scores on a very easy test. Most students score 90 to 100.
    {{a}}Skewed left. Most scores are high. A few students score low.{{/a}}
  3. The heights of all adult women in Missouri.
    {{a}}Symmetric. Most heights are near the middle, with about as many tall as short.{{/a}}
8. Look at the US household income graph on page 1.
  1. Why is the mean (\$121,000) bigger than the median (\$84,000)?
    {{a}}Income is skewed right. A small number of very high incomes pull the mean toward the tail.{{/a}}
  2. A news story says, “The average US household earns \$121,000.” Is this true? Does it describe a typical household? Explain.
    {{a}}It is true for the mean. It does not describe a typical household. Most households earn less. The median is a better description of a typical household.{{/a}}
  3. Do more than half of households earn less than \$121,000? How do you know?
    {{a}}Yes. Half of households earn less than \$84,000 (the median). Some more earn between \$84,000 and \$121,000.{{/a}}
