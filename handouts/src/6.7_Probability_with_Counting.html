When every outcome is equally likely, count the outcomes with permutations or combinations.

$P(\text{event}) = \dfrac{\text{number of ways the event can happen}}{\text{total number of possible outcomes}}$    Count both with the same method.

Example 1: choosing a group. A teacher randomly picks 2 students from 5 girls and 4 boys. What is $P(\text{both girls})$?

Total ways to pick 2 of 9:${}_9C_2 = 36$
Ways to pick 2 of the 5 girls:${}_5C_2 = 10$
Probability:$P(\text{both girls}) = \dfrac{10}{36} = \dfrac{5}{18} \approx 0.28$

Example 2: a lottery. Pick 3 different numbers from 1 to 20. Order does not matter. What is $P(\text{win})$?

Total: ${}_{20}C_3 = 1{,}140$.   Only 1 of them wins.   $P(\text{win}) = \dfrac{1}{1{,}140} \approx 0.0009$

Example 3: jobs. 10 students are in a club. A president and a vice president are chosen at random. What is the probability that Ana is president and Ben is vice president?

Order matters (different jobs). Total: ${}_{10}P_2 = 90$.   Only 1 way works.   $P = \dfrac{1}{90}$

Steps

1.Decide: does order matter? (permutation or combination)
2.Count all the possible outcomes.
3.Count the outcomes you want.
4.Divide.

1. A committee of 3 is chosen at random from 6 juniors (11th grade) and 4 seniors (12th grade).
  1. How many committees are possible?  {{a}}${}_{10}C_3 = 120${{/a}}
  2. Find $P(\text{all seniors})$.
    {{a}}${}_4C_3 = 4$.  $\frac{4}{120} = \frac{1}{30}${{/a}}
  3. Find $P(\text{all juniors})$.
    {{a}}${}_6C_3 = 20$.  $\frac{20}{120} = \frac{1}{6}${{/a}}
2. A lottery: pick 4 different numbers from 1 to 30. Order does not matter. What is the probability of winning with one ticket?
{{a}}${}_{30}C_4 = 27{,}405$.  $P(\text{win}) = \frac{1}{27{,}405}${{/a}}
3. 6 runners are in a race. Each order of finish is equally likely. What is the probability that Maya finishes 1st and Leo finishes 2nd?
{{a}}Order matters. ${}_6P_2 = 30$.  $P = \frac{1}{30}${{/a}}
4. Two cards are dealt from a deck of 52. What is $P(\text{both aces})$? (There are 4 aces.)
{{a}}${}_4C_2 = 6$ and ${}_{52}C_2 = 1{,}326$.  $P = \frac{6}{1{,}326} = \frac{1}{221}${{/a}}

5. In a taste test, there are 4 drinks from 4 different brands. A person tastes each drink and matches it to a brand. Each brand is used once. If the person just guesses, what is the probability that all 4 are right?
{{a}}There are $4! = 24$ ways to match the brands. Only 1 is right.  $P = \frac{1}{24}${{/a}}
6. You grab 2 shirts at random from a drawer of 8 shirts. 3 of the shirts are blue. What is $P(\text{both blue})$?
{{a}}${}_3C_2 = 3$ and ${}_8C_2 = 28$.  $P = \frac{3}{28}${{/a}}
7. A big lottery asks you to pick 5 numbers from 1 to 70 and 1 extra number from 1 to 25.
  1. Use a calculator. How many different tickets are possible?
    {{a}}${}_{70}C_5 \cdot 25 = 12{,}103{,}014 \cdot 25 = 302{,}575{,}350${{/a}}
  2. What is the probability of winning with one ticket? Should you expect to win? Explain.
    {{a}}$\frac{1}{302{,}575{,}350}$. No. Winning is extremely unlikely.{{/a}}
