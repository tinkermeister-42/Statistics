Ten students recorded how many hours they studied and their test score. A scatterplot shows both numbers for each student. Each dot is one student.

{{fig Unit_4/4.1_study_line w=3.43in}}
DirectionPositive: as $x$ goes up, $y$ goes up. (Negative: $y$ goes down. No pattern at all: no association.)
FormLinear: the dots follow a line. (Or curved.)
StrengthStrong: the dots are close to the line. (Or moderate: a little spread out. Or weak: very spread out.)
UnusualOutliers or clusters? None here.

Line of best fit. A line that follows the trend of the dots.

  • It goes through the middle of the dots.
  • About the same number of dots are above it as below it.

Finding its equation. Pick two points on the line (they do not have to be dots): $(2, 64)$ and $(6, 84)$.

Slope:$m = \dfrac{84 - 64}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{20}{4} = 5$
$y$-intercept:$64 = 5(2) + b$,  so $b = 54$
Equation:$\hat{y} = 5x + 54$

$\hat{y}$ is read “y-hat.” The hat means predicted. The line predicts $y$; it does not give the exact $y$.

Predicting. A student studies 5 hours:  $\hat{y} = 5(5) + 54 = 79$.  The predicted score is 79.

Gotcha: extrapolation
Predicting far outside the data is risky. 12 hours gives $\hat{y} = 5(12) + 54 = 114$, but a test score cannot be more than 100. The data only goes from 1 to 8 hours.
