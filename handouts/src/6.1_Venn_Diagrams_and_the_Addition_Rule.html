Probability is how likely something is, from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). When every outcome is equally likely:

$$P(A) = \frac{\text{number of ways } A \text{ can happen}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}$$

$P(A)$ is read “the probability of $A$.”    Complement: $P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)$.  (Some books write $P(A^C)$.)

Example: A bag has 3 red and 7 blue marbles. Pick one.  $P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{3}{10}$, and $P(\text{not red}) = 1 - \dfrac{3}{10} = \dfrac{7}{10}$.

Symbols for “or” and “and”

SymbolRead it asMeaning
$A \cup B$“$A$ union $B$” or “$A$ or $B$”$A$ happens, or $B$ happens, or both
$A \cap B$“$A$ intersect $B$” or “$A$ and $B$”$A$ and $B$ both happen (the overlap)

Example: 30 students. 12 play soccer, 10 play basketball, 4 play both.

{{fig Unit_6/6.1_venn_sports w=2.4in}}

If we add $12 + 10$, the 4 students who play both are counted twice. Subtract them once.

$P(S \cup B) = \dfrac{12}{30} + \dfrac{10}{30} - \dfrac{4}{30} = \dfrac{18}{30} = 0.6$

The addition rule:    $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$

Mutually exclusive events cannot both happen, so $P(A \cap B) = 0$. Then $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$.

Example: Roll a number cube (1 to 6). Rolling a 1 and rolling a 2 are mutually exclusive.

$P(1 \cup 2) = \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

A deck of cards has 52 cards: 4 suits (hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades), 13 cards in each suit (A, 2 to 10, J, Q, K).
