A line of best fit makes predictions. Most real points are a little above or below the line. A residual measures how far off the prediction is.

residual $=$ actual $-$ predicted $= y - \hat{y}$
Positive residual: the point is above the line.     Negative residual: the point is below the line.
{{fig Unit_4/4.3_residuals w=3.54in}}

Example: The line is $\hat{y} = 5x + 54$. Two students studied 4 hours.

$x$$y$ (actual)$\hat{y}$ (predicted)$y - \hat{y}$Meaning
478$5(4) + 54 = 74$$78 - 74 = 4$scored 4 points more than predicted
47274$72 - 74 = {-2}$scored 2 points less than predicted

The least-squares line. Square every residual and add them: $\Sigma(y - \hat{y})^2$. The least-squares regression line is the line that makes this sum as small as possible. Calculators and computers find this line for you.

Residual plots. Put $x$ across the bottom and the residuals up the side.

2. Use the study line $\hat{y} = 5x + 54$.
  1. A student studied 6 hours and scored 90. Find the residual.
    {{a}}$\hat{y} = 5(6) + 54 = 84$.  Residual $= 90 - 84 = 6$.{{/a}}
  2. Another student has a residual of $-5$. What does that mean?
    {{a}}The student scored 5 points less than the line predicted.{{/a}}
3. The used car line is $\hat{y} = {-2.5}x + 30$ (value in thousands of dollars). A 6-year-old car is worth \$14,000. Find the residual and explain it.
{{a}}$\hat{y} = {-2.5}(6) + 30 = 15$.  Residual $= 14 - 15 = {-1}$. The car is worth \$1,000 less than predicted.{{/a}}
