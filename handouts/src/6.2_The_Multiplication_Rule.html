You guess on 3 multiple-choice questions. Each has 4 choices. What is the probability you get all 3 right? Each guess has a $\dfrac{1}{4}$ chance. The questions do not affect each other, so multiply:

$$P(\text{all 3 right}) = \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{64}$$

Independent events. Two events are independent if one happening does not change the probability of the other.

Independent:    $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$

Example: Flip a coin and roll a number cube.   $P(\text{heads} \cap 6) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{1}{12}$

Dependent events. Two events are dependent if one happening changes the probability of the other. A bag has 5 red and 3 blue marbles. Draw 2 marbles.

With replacement (put the first one back)Without replacement (keep the first one out)
The second draw is the same as the first.
Independent.		 The bag changes: 4 red and 3 blue are left.
Dependent.
$P(\text{red, red}) = \dfrac{5}{8} \cdot \dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{25}{64}$ $P(\text{red, red}) = \dfrac{5}{8} \cdot \dfrac{4}{7} = \dfrac{20}{56} = \dfrac{5}{14}$

General multiplication rule:    $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A)$

$P(B \mid A)$ is read “the probability of $B$ given $A$.” It is the probability of $B$ after $A$ has already happened.

In the marble example: $P(\text{red second} \mid \text{red first}) = \dfrac{4}{7}$.

Tip
“At least one” questions are often easier with the complement. $P(\text{at least one wrong}) = 1 - P(\text{all right})$.

  1. $P(\text{all 5 right})$  {{a}}$\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^5 = \frac{1}{32}${{/a}}

3. A bag has 4 green and 6 yellow marbles. Draw 2 marbles.
  1. Find $P(\text{green, green})$ with replacement.
    {{a}}$\frac{4}{10} \cdot \frac{4}{10} = \frac{16}{100} = \frac{4}{25}${{/a}}
  2. Find $P(\text{green, green})$ without replacement.
    {{a}}$\frac{4}{10} \cdot \frac{3}{9} = \frac{12}{90} = \frac{2}{15}${{/a}}
  3. Why is the answer to (b) smaller?
    {{a}}After a green is taken out, fewer greens are left, so the second green is less likely.{{/a}}

4. Independent or dependent? Explain.
  1. Flip a coin, then roll a number cube.
    {{a}}Independent. The coin does not change the cube.{{/a}}
  2. Pick a class president, then pick a vice president from the students who are left.
    {{a}}Dependent. The president cannot be picked again, so the choices change.{{/a}}
  3. Draw a card, put it back, shuffle, and draw again.
    {{a}}Independent. The deck is the same for the second draw.{{/a}}
  4. It rains today, and you carry an umbrella today.
    {{a}}Dependent. Rain makes carrying an umbrella more likely.{{/a}}

  1. Find $P(\text{late} \cap \text{detention})$.  {{a}}$0.2 \cdot 0.5 = 0.1${{/a}}
  2. Explain what your answer means in words.
    {{a}}About 10% of all students are late and get a detention.{{/a}}
