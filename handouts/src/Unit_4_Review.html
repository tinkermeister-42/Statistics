One data set, every tool. 8 candles of the same kind burned for different numbers of hours. Then each candle was measured.

Hours burned ($x$)12345678
Height in cm ($y$)23191817131398

4.1 Describe and fit.

Direction, form, strength.
A line of best fit goes through the middle of the dots.
{{fig Unit_4/Review_candles left}}

Negative, linear, strong.

Two points on the line:
$(2, 20)$ and $(7, 10)$.

$m = \dfrac{10 - 20}{7 - 2} = \dfrac{-10}{5} = {-2}$

$20 = {-2}(2) + b$,  so $b = 24$

$\hat{y} = {-2}x + 24$

Predict for 4.5 hours:  $\hat{y} = {-2}(4.5) + 24 = 15$ cm

Predict for 15 hours:  $\hat{y} = {-2}(15) + 24 = {-6}$ cm. A height cannot be negative. The data only goes from 1 to 8 hours. This is extrapolation.

4.2 Explain the line.

Slope: for each additional [$x$ unit], the predicted [$y$] changes by the slope.
$r$ is from $-1$ to $1$.

Slope ($-2$): For each additional hour, the predicted height decreases by 2 cm.

y-intercept (24): When $x = 0$ (a new candle), the predicted height is 24 cm.

$r$: The dots are close to a line that goes down. $r$ is close to $-1$ ($r \approx {-0.98}$).

4.3 Find a residual.

residual
$=$ actual $-$ predicted
$= y - \hat{y}$
Positive: above the line.
Negative: below the line.

The candle that burned 4 hours is 17 cm.  $\hat{y} = {-2}(4) + 24 = 16$ cm

Residual $= 17 - 16 = 1$. It is positive, so the dot is above the line. The candle is 1 cm taller than predicted.

The least-squares line makes $\Sigma(y - \hat{y})^2$ as small as possible.

{{fig Unit_4/Review_candles_resid left}}

The residual plot shows no pattern. A line is a good model.

Correlation is not causation. When more umbrellas are sold, there are more car accidents. Umbrellas do not cause accidents. Rain causes both. Rain is a lurking variable.
