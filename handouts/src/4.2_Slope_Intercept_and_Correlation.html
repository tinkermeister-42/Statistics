A student measured a plant every week. The line of best fit is $\hat{y} = 2.5x + 4$.

{{fig Unit_4/4.2_plant w=3.05in}}
Slope (2.5): For each additional week, the predicted height increases by 2.5 cm.
y-intercept (4): When $x = 0$ (the day it was planted), the predicted height is 4 cm.

Sentence frames

Slope:For each additional [$x$ unit], the predicted [$y$] increases (or decreases) by [slope and units].
Intercept:When [$x$] is 0, the predicted [$y$] is [intercept and units].
Gotcha
Sometimes the intercept has no real meaning. If $x$ is a person's height, $x = 0$ is not possible.

The correlation coefficient $r$ measures how strong a linear pattern is.

{{fig Unit_4/4.2_r_examples}}
$r$ is always from $-1$ to $1$.
Sign shows the direction:$r > 0$ positive,  $r < 0$ negative.
Size shows the strength:close to $1$ or $-1$ is strong. Close to $0$ is weak.
$r = {-0.8}$ is stronger than $r = 0.6$.The sign only tells the direction.

Correlation is not causation. In summer, ice cream sales and sunburns both go up. They have a strong positive correlation. Ice cream does not cause sunburns. Hot, sunny weather causes both. A hidden cause like this is called a lurking variable.

{{practice-head}}
1. The value of a used car is $\hat{y} = {-2.5}x + 30$, where $x$ is the age in years and $y$ is the value in thousands of dollars.
  1. Explain the slope in context.
    {{a}}For each additional year of age, the predicted value decreases by \$2,500 (2.5 thousand dollars).{{/a}}
  2. Explain the y-intercept in context.
    {{a}}When the car is new (0 years old), the predicted value is \$30,000.{{/a}}
2. For adults, arm span can be predicted from height: $\hat{y} = 1.02x - 1.3$, where $x$ and $y$ are in inches.
  1. Explain the slope in context.
    {{a}}For each additional inch of height, the predicted arm span increases by 1.02 inches.{{/a}}
  2. Does the y-intercept have a real meaning here? Explain.
    {{a}}No. It says a person 0 inches tall has an arm span of $-1.3$ inches. No adult is 0 inches tall.{{/a}}
3. Match each $r$ to a scatterplot:   $r = {-0.9}$,  $r = 0.3$,  $r = 0.85$,  $r = {-0.4}$
{{fig Unit_4/4.2_match}}
{{a}}P: $r = 0.85$.   Q: $r = {-0.4}$.   R: $r = 0.3$.   S: $r = {-0.9}$.{{/a}}

4. Is each statement true or false? Explain.
  1. $r = {-0.9}$ shows a weaker relationship than $r = 0.5$.
    {{a}}False. $-0.9$ is closer to $-1$, so it is stronger. The minus sign only means negative.{{/a}}
  2. A scatterplot has $r = 1.3$.
    {{a}}False. $r$ is always from $-1$ to $1$.{{/a}}
  3. $r = 0$ means there is no linear pattern.
    {{a}}True. (There could still be a curved pattern.){{/a}}
5. In a city, the number of fire trucks sent to a fire and the amount of damage have a strong positive correlation.
  1. Do more fire trucks cause more damage? Explain.
    {{a}}No. Correlation does not prove cause.{{/a}}
  2. What lurking variable could cause both?
    {{a}}The size of the fire. A big fire causes more damage and gets more trucks.{{/a}}
6. A study finds that students who eat breakfast have higher grades. A news story says, “Eating breakfast raises your grades.” Is the news story right? Name a lurking variable that could explain the pattern.
{{a}}Not proven. This only shows an association. Lurking variables: more sleep, a family routine, or more support at home could lead to both breakfast and better grades.{{/a}}
