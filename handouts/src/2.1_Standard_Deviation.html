Two basketball players both score 10 points per game on average.

{{fig Unit_2/2.1_players w=5.2in}}

Player A's points are close to the mean. Player B's points are spread far from the mean. The standard deviation (SD) measures this.

Standard deviation is about how far the values usually are from the mean.
Small SD: values are close to the mean.    Large SD: values are spread out.

How to Find the Standard Deviation

Example: 7, 7, 10, 13, 13

1.Find the mean, $\bar{x}$.   $\bar{x} = 50 \div 5 = 10$
2.Subtract the mean from each value.
3.Square each result.
4.Add the squares.   $\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2 = 36$
5.Divide by $n - 1$.   $36 \div 4 = 9$
(This number is called the variance.)
6.Take the square root.   $s = \sqrt{9} = \mathbf{3}$

The formula:   $\displaystyle s = \sqrt{\frac{\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}$    ($s$ = the standard deviation,  $n$ = how many values)

$\Sigma$ (capital sigma) means add up.  $\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2$ means: find $(x - \bar{x})^2$ for every value, then add them all.

Why $n - 1$?
Our data is usually a sample, a small part of a bigger group. A sample is usually a little less spread out than the whole group. Dividing by $n - 1$ makes $s$ a little bigger to fix this.
Remember
  • The SD is never negative.
  • The SD is 0 only when every value is the same.
  • The SD has the same units as the data (points, minutes, inches).
