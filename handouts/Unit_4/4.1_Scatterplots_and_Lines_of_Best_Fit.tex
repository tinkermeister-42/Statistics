\lessontitle{Scatterplots and Lines of Best Fit}

Ten students recorded how many hours they studied and their test score. A \textbf{scatterplot} shows both numbers for each student. Each dot is one student.

\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.5\linewidth}\centering
\scalebox{0.92}{\fig{Unit_4/4.1_study_line}}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.47\linewidth}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.85in}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{2.25in}}
\textbf{Direction} & \textbf{Positive}: as $x$ goes up, $y$ goes up. (Negative: $y$ goes down. No pattern at all: no association.)\\
\textbf{Form} & \textbf{Linear}: the dots follow a line. (Or curved.)\\
\textbf{Strength} & \textbf{Strong}: the dots are close to the line. (Or moderate: loosely around it. Or weak: spread out.)\\
\textbf{Unusual} & Outliers or clusters? None here.\\
\end{tabular}
\end{minipage}

\vspace{0.8em}
\textbf{Line of best fit.} A line that follows the trend of the dots.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item It goes through the middle of the dots.
  \item About the same number of dots are above it as below it.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{Finding its equation.} Pick two points \textbf{on the line} (they do not have to be dots): $(2, 64)$ and $(6, 84)$.

\vspace{0.3em}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll}
Slope: & $m = \dfrac{84 - 64}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{20}{4} = 5$\\
$y$-intercept: & $64 = 5(2) + b$, \; so $b = 54$\\
Equation: & $\hat{y} = 5x + 54$\\
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.3em}
$\hat{y}$ is read ``y-hat.'' The hat means \textbf{predicted}. The line predicts $y$; it does not give the exact $y$.

\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{Predicting.} A student studies 5 hours: \; $\hat{y} = 5(5) + 54 = 79$. \; The predicted score is 79.

\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{Gotcha: extrapolation.} Predicting far outside the data is risky.
12 hours gives $\hat{y} = 5(12) + 54 = 114$, but a test score cannot be more than 100.
The data only goes from 1 to 8 hours.

\item This scatterplot shows the age and value of 10 used cars. A line of best fit is drawn.
\begin{center}\scalebox{0.9}{\fig{Unit_4/4.1_car}}\end{center}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em]
  \item Describe the direction, form, and strength. \ans{\quad Negative, linear, strong}
  \work{1.5em}
  \item Use the two marked points to find the slope.

  \ans{$m = \dfrac{10 - 30}{8 - 0} = \dfrac{-20}{8} = -2.5$}
  \work{3em}
  \item Write the equation of the line. \ans{\quad $\hat{y} = -2.5x + 30$}
  \work{1.5em}
  \item Predict the value of a 6-year-old car.

  \ans{$\hat{y} = -2.5(6) + 30 = 15$, \; about \$15,000}
  \work{2.5em}
  \item Predict the value of a 15-year-old car. Does your answer make sense? Explain.

  \ans{$\hat{y} = -2.5(15) + 30 = -7.5$. No. A car cannot be worth less than \$0. 15 years is far outside the data (1 to 9 years).}
\end{enumerate}
\work{2.5em}

\item The line for the study data is $\hat{y} = 5x + 54$.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em]
  \item Predict the score of a student who studies 3 hours. \ans{\quad $5(3) + 54 = 69$}
  \work{1.5em}
  \item A student plans to study 10 hours. Predict the score. Should you trust this prediction? Explain.

  \ans{$5(10) + 54 = 104$. No. Scores cannot be more than 100, and 10 hours is outside the data.}
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
