7.3  Expected Payoff

Mini Lesson - Unit 7: Probability Distributions - DHS Statistics
NameDatePeriod

Is a lottery ticket worth buying? Is a protection plan worth the money? The expected value of the payoff tells you what happens on average.

Net gain == what you win −- what you paid
Expected payoff: let XX = the net gain. Then E(X)=Σx⋅P(x)E(X) = \Sigma\, x \cdot P(x).

Example 1: a lottery ticket. A ticket costs $2. 1 in 1,000 tickets wins $500. 1 in 50 tickets wins $10. The rest win nothing.

OutcomeNet gain xxP(x)P(x)x⋅P(x)x \cdot P(x)
Win $500500−2=498500 - 2 = 4980.0010.498
Win $1010−2=810 - 2 = 80.020.16
Win nothing−2-20.979−1.958-1.958
E(X)=Σx⋅P(x)E(X) = \Sigma\, x \cdot P(x)−1.30-1.30

On average, a buyer loses $1.30 per ticket. The lottery keeps that money.

Example 2: a protection plan. A phone costs $300 to replace. The chance it breaks this year is 0.10. A protection plan costs $40 for the year.

Expected cost with no plan:0.10⋅300=$300.10 \cdot 300 = $30
Cost of the plan:$40

On average, the plan costs $10 more than paying for a broken phone yourself. That is how the company makes money.

But: the plan protects you from a sudden $300 bill. Expected value is one part of a decision. Risk is another part: could you pay the big bill if it came? If not, the plan might be worth it to you.
