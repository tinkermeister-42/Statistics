\lessontitle{Probability Distributions}

Flip a coin 2 times. Let $X$ = the number of heads. $X$ is a \textbf{random variable}: a number that comes from a chance process.

\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.52\linewidth}
Outcomes: HH, HT, TH, TT (all equally likely)

\vspace{0.6em}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}\hline
$x$ (heads) & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ \hline
$P(x)$ & $\frac{1}{4}$ & $\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$ & $\frac{1}{4}$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.6em}
This table is the \textbf{probability distribution} of $X$. It lists every value of $X$ and its probability.

\vspace{0.4em}
$P(x)$ means the probability that $X$ equals $x$. Capital $X$ is the random variable. Small $x$ is one of its values.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.44\linewidth}\centering
\scalebox{0.8}{\fig{Unit_7/7.1_two_coins}}
\end{minipage}

\vspace{0.8em}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{6.7in}|}\hline
\textbf{A valid probability distribution:}\par
1. Every probability is from 0 to 1. \qquad 2. The probabilities add to 1: \; $\Sigma P(x) = 1$\\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.3em}
Check: $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = 1$. \quad Valid.

\vspace{0.8em}
\textbf{Using a distribution.} Add the probabilities of the values you want.

\vspace{0.2em}
$P(X \ge 1) = P(1) + P(2) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$ \qquad ($\ge$ means ``greater than or equal to'')

\vspace{1em}
\textbf{Theoretical or empirical?}

\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{tabular}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{1.4in}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{5.1in}|}\hline
\textbf{Theoretical} & From reasoning about the chance process (equally likely outcomes, or the multiplication rule), like the coin flips above.\\ \hline
\textbf{Empirical} & From data. Divide each count by the total. \par
50 families: 0 cars: 5, \; 1 car: 20, \; 2 cars: 18, \; 3 cars: 7\par
$P(0) = \frac{5}{50} = 0.10$, \; $P(1) = 0.40$, \; $P(2) = 0.36$, \; $P(3) = 0.14$\\[0.3em] \hline
\end{tabular}

\item Is each table a valid probability distribution? Explain.

\vspace{0.3em}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll}
(a) \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}\hline $x$ & 1 & 2 & 3\\ \hline $P(x)$ & 0.2 & 0.3 & 0.5\\ \hline\end{tabular} & \qquad &
(b) \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}\hline $x$ & 1 & 2 & 3\\ \hline $P(x)$ & 0.4 & 0.4 & 0.3\\ \hline\end{tabular}\\[2.6em]
(c) \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}\hline $x$ & 1 & 2 & 3\\ \hline $P(x)$ & 0.5 & $-0.1$ & 0.6\\ \hline\end{tabular} & &
(d) \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}\hline $x$ & 1 & 2 & 3\\ \hline $P(x)$ & $\frac{1}{3}$ & $\frac{1}{3}$ & $\frac{1}{3}$\\ \hline\end{tabular}\\
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.5em}
\ans{(a) Valid: sums to 1. \; (b) Not valid: sums to 1.1. \; (c) Not valid: a probability cannot be negative. \\ (d) Valid: each is from 0 to 1, and they sum to 1.}
\work{6em}

\item Find the missing probability.

\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline $x$ & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3\\ \hline $P(x)$ & 0.15 & 0.35 & ? & 0.10\\ \hline\end{tabular}
\qquad \ans{$1 - (0.15 + 0.35 + 0.10) = 0.40$}
\work{3em}

\item A player makes 70\% of her free throws. She shoots 2. Her shots do not affect each other. Let $X$ = the number she makes. Make the probability distribution of $X$.

$x$ & 0 & 1 & 2\\ \hline
