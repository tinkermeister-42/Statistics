\lessontitle{Combinations}

Pick 3 students from 5 (A, B, C, D, E) to be on a committee. All 3 have the same job.
Choosing A, B, C is the \textbf{same committee} as choosing C, A, B. \textbf{Order does not matter.}

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll}
Count as if order mattered: & ${}_5P_3 = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 60$\\
Each committee of 3 was counted $3! = 6$ times: & ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA\\
Divide: & $60 \div 6 = \mathbf{10}$ committees\\
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.8em}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{6.7in}|}\hline
\rule{0pt}{2.4em}\textbf{Combination formula:} \qquad ${}_nC_r = \dbinom{n}{r} = \dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n - r)!}$ \qquad choose $r$ things from $n$ things\par\vspace{0.4em}
${}_nC_r$ and $\binom{n}{r}$ are both read ``$n$ choose $r$.'' Some books write $C(n, r)$.\\[0.4em] \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Example:} \qquad ${}_5C_3 = \dfrac{5!}{3!\,2!} = \dfrac{120}{6 \cdot 2} = 10$

\vspace{1em}
\textbf{Permutation or combination?} Ask: \textit{If I switch the order, is it a different result?}

\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{tabular}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.2in}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.3in}|}\hline
\textbf{Order matters: permutation} & \textbf{Order does not matter: combination}\\ \hline
President, VP, secretary (different jobs) & A committee of 3 (same job)\\ \hline
1st, 2nd, 3rd place & Picking 3 winners of the same prize\\ \hline
Arranging books on a shelf & Choosing 3 books to take on a trip\\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.8em}
\textbf{Handshakes.} 10 people each shake hands once with everyone else. A handshake is a pair, and Ana-Ben is the same as Ben-Ana:
\qquad ${}_{10}C_2 = \dfrac{10 \cdot 9}{2} = 45$ handshakes.

% ---------------- PROBLEMS ----------------
\newpage
\begin{enumerate}

\item Find each value.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1.4em]
  \item ${}_5C_2$ \ans{\quad $\frac{5 \cdot 4}{2 \cdot 1} = 10$}
  \item ${}_6C_3$ \ans{\quad $\frac{6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 20$}
  \item ${}_8C_1$ \ans{\quad 8}
  \item $\binom{7}{7}$ \ans{\quad 1 (there is one way to choose all of them)}
\end{enumerate}
\work{1em}

\item Permutation or combination? Then find the number of ways.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1.8em]
  \item Choose 3 pizza toppings from 10. \ans{\quad Combination. ${}_{10}C_3 = 120$}
  \item Give 1st, 2nd, and 3rd prizes to 3 of 10 people. \ans{\quad Permutation. ${}_{10}P_3 = 720$}
  \item Choose a president and a treasurer from 12 people. \ans{\quad Permutation. ${}_{12}P_2 = 132$}
  \item Choose 4 people from 15 for a committee. \ans{\quad Combination. ${}_{15}C_4 = 1{,}365$}
\end{enumerate}
\work{1em}

\item 12 people meet. Each person shakes hands once with every other person. How many handshakes?

\ans{${}_{12}C_2 = \frac{12 \cdot 11}{2} = 66$}
\work{3em}

\newpage
\item A restaurant has 8 toppings. A pizza has 2 different toppings. How many different 2-topping pizzas are there?

\ans{${}_8C_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 7}{2} = 28$}
\work{3em}

\item Compare ${}_{10}P_3 = 720$ and ${}_{10}C_3 = 120$.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em]
  \item Why is ${}_{10}P_3$ bigger? \ans{\quad It counts each group of 3 once for every order.}
  \work{1.5em}
  \item What number do you divide by to get from 720 to 120? Why? \ans{\quad $3! = 6$. Each group of 3 can be ordered 6 ways.}
\end{enumerate}
\work{2em}

\item A hand of 5 cards is dealt from a deck of 52 cards. Use a calculator. How many different hands are there?

\ans{${}_{52}C_5 = 2{,}598{,}960$}
\work{3em}

\item A student says, ``Choosing 2 of 6 students to clean the board is a permutation, because there are 2 people.''
Do you agree? Explain.

\ans{No. Both students do the same job, so the order does not matter. It is a combination: ${}_6C_2 = 15$.}

\end{enumerate}
