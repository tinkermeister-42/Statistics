6.6  Combinations

Mini Lesson - Unit 6: Probability - DHS Statistics
Teacher Key: answers in red

Pick 3 students from 5 (A, B, C, D, E) to be on a committee. All 3 have the same job. Choosing A, B, C is the same committee as choosing C, A, B. Order does not matter.

Count as if order mattered:5P3=5⋅4⋅3=60{}_5P_3 = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 60
Each committee of 3 was counted 3!=63! = 6 times:ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA
Divide:60÷6=𝟏𝟎60 \div 6 = \mathbf{10} committees

Combination formula:    nCr=(nr)=n!r!(n−r)!{}_nC_r = \left.\displaystyle\binom{n}{r}\right. = \dfrac{n!}{r!\,\mathopen{(}n - r\mathclose{)}!}    choose rr things from nn things

nCr{}_nC_r and (nr)\left.\displaystyle\binom{n}{r}\right. are both read “nn choose rr.” Some books write C(n,r)C(n, r).

Example:    5C3=5!3!2!=1206⋅2=10{}_5C_3 = \dfrac{5!}{3!\,2!} = \dfrac{120}{6 \cdot 2} = 10

Permutation or combination? Ask: If I switch the order, is it a different result?

Order matters: permutationOrder does not matter: combination
President, vice president, secretary (different jobs)A committee of 3 (same job)
1st, 2nd, 3rd placePicking 3 winners of the same prize
Arranging books on a shelfChoosing 3 books to take on a trip

Handshakes. 10 people each shake hands once with everyone else. A handshake is a pair, and Ana-Ben is the same as Ben-Ana:    10C2=10⋅92=45{}_{10}C_2 = \dfrac{10 \cdot 9}{2} = 45 handshakes.

3. 12 people meet. Each person shakes hands once with every other person. How many handshakes?
12C2=12⋅112=66{}_{12}C_2 = \frac{12 \cdot 11}{2} = 66
4. A restaurant has 8 toppings. A pizza has 2 different toppings. How many different 2-topping pizzas are there?
8C2=8⋅72=28{}_8C_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 7}{2} = 28
5. Compare 10P3=720{}_{10}P_3 = 720 and 10C3=120{}_{10}C_3 = 120.
  1. Why is 10P3{}_{10}P_3 bigger?  It counts each group of 3 once for every order.
  2. What number do you divide by to get from 720 to 120? Why?  3!=63! = 6. Each group of 3 can be ordered 6 ways.
6. A hand of 5 cards is dealt from a deck of 52 cards. Use a calculator. How many different hands are there?
52C5=2,598,960{}_{52}C_5 = 2{,}598{,}960
7. A student says, “Choosing 2 of 6 students to clean the board is a permutation, because there are 2 people.” Do you agree? Explain.
No. Both students do the same job, so the order does not matter. It is a combination: 6C2=15{}_6C_2 = 15.
