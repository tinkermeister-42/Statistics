Probability Symbols

SymbolRead it asMeaning
P(A)P(A)“the probability of AA”How likely AA is, from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).
P(not A)P(\text{not } A)“the probability of not AA”The complement: AA does not happen. Some books write P(AC)P(A^C).
A∪BA \cup B“AA union BB” or “AA or BB”AA happens, or BB happens, or both.
A∩BA \cap B“AA intersect BB” or “AA and BB”AA and BB both happen (the overlap).
P(A∣B)P(A \mid B)“the probability of AA given BB”The probability of AA when you know BB happened. The bar ∣\mid means “given.”

Checking independence AA and BB are independent if P(A∣B)=P(A)P(A \mid B) = P(A). Knowing BB does not change the probability of AA. P(job)=2060≈0.33P(\text{job}) = \frac{20}{60} \approx 0.33, but P(job∣sport)≈0.27P(\text{job} \mid \text{sport}) \approx 0.27. Not independent.

Independent: one happening does not change the probability of the other, as in drawing with replacement. Dependent: one happening changes it, as in drawing without replacement.

Simulations

IdeaWhat it meansExample
Theoretical probability Found by reasoning about equally likely outcomes. Flip a coin. P(heads)=12P(\text{heads}) = \frac{1}{2}
Experimental probability Found by doing trials: number of times it happenednumber of trials\dfrac{\text{number of times it happened}}{\text{number of trials}} 20 flips, 12 heads: 1220=0.6\frac{12}{20} = 0.6
Simulation Use random numbers to act out a chance event many times.
1. Assign digits to the outcomes so the probabilities match.
2. Decide what one trial is.
3. Run many trials with random digits.
4. Count how often the event happened.		 A basketball player makes 70% of her free throws. About how often does she make all 3 of 3 shots?
1. Digits 0 to 6 = make. 7, 8, 9 = miss.
2. One trial = 3 digits (3 shots).
3. Run 10 trials.
4. 4 of the 10 made all 3: 410=0.4\frac{4}{10} = 0.4
Law of large numbers The more trials you do, the closer the experimental probability usually gets to the theoretical probability. The theoretical probability of making all 3 shots is 0.7⋅0.7⋅0.7=0.3430.7 \cdot 0.7 \cdot 0.7 = 0.343. With more trials, the simulation would get closer to it.
