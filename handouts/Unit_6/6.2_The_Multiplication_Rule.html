6.2  The Multiplication Rule

Mini Lesson - Unit 6: Probability - DHS Statistics
NameDatePeriod

You guess on 3 multiple-choice questions. Each has 4 choices. What is the probability you get all 3 right? Each guess has a 14\dfrac{1}{4} chance. The questions do not affect each other, so multiply:

P(all 3 right)=14⋅14⋅14=164P(\text{all 3 right}) = \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{64}

Independent events. Two events are independent if one happening does not change the probability of the other.

Independent:    P(A∩B)=P(A)⋅P(B)P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)

Example: Flip a coin and roll a number cube.   P(heads∩6)=12⋅16=112P(\text{heads} \cap 6) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{1}{12}

Dependent events. Two events are dependent if one happening changes the probability of the other. A bag has 5 red and 3 blue marbles. Draw 2 marbles.

With replacement (put the first one back)Without replacement (keep the first one out)
The second draw is the same as the first.
Independent.		 The bag changes: 4 red and 3 blue are left.
Dependent.
P(red, red)=58⋅58=2564P(\text{red, red}) = \dfrac{5}{8} \cdot \dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{25}{64} P(red, red)=58⋅47=2056=514P(\text{red, red}) = \dfrac{5}{8} \cdot \dfrac{4}{7} = \dfrac{20}{56} = \dfrac{5}{14}

General multiplication rule:    P(A∩B)=P(A)⋅P(B∣A)P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A)

P(B∣A)P(B \mid A) is read “the probability of BB given AA.” It is the probability of BB after AA has already happened.

In the marble example: P(red second∣red first)=47P(\text{red second} \mid \text{red first}) = \dfrac{4}{7}.

Tip
“At least one” questions are often easier with the complement. P(at least one wrong)=1−P(all right)P(\text{at least one wrong}) = 1 - P(\text{all right}).
