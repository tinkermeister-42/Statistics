6.5  Permutations

Mini Lesson - Unit 6: Probability - DHS Statistics
Teacher Key: answers in red

The counting principle. If one choice has mm options and a second choice has nn options, there are m⋅nm \cdot n ways to make both choices.

Example: 3 shirts and 4 pairs of pants make 3⋅4=123 \cdot 4 = 12 outfits.

Arranging. In how many ways can 4 students sit in 4 chairs in a row?

Chair 1Chair 2Chair 3Chair 4
4 choices3 left2 left1 left

4⋅3⋅2⋅1=244 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 24 ways

Factorial:   n!=n⋅(n−1)⋅(n−2)⋯2⋅1n! = n \cdot (n - 1) \cdot (n - 2) \cdots 2 \cdot 1    read “nn factorial”

4!=4⋅3⋅2⋅1=244! = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 24    1!=11! = 1    0!=10! = 1 (there is exactly one way to arrange nothing)

Permutations. A permutation is an arrangement where order matters.

Example: A club of 10 students picks a president, a vice president, and a secretary. How many ways?

10⋅9⋅8=72010 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 = 720 ways.   (Ana as president and Ben as vice president is different from Ben as president and Ana as vice president.)

Permutation formula:    nPr=n!(n−r)!{}_nP_r = \dfrac{n!}{(n - r)!}    arrange rr things chosen from nn things

10P3=10!7!={}_{10}P_3 = \dfrac{10!}{7!} = 10 · 9 · 8 · 7 · 6 ⋯ 17 · 6 ⋯ 1 =720= 720

nPr{}_nP_r is read “nn permute rr.” Some books write P(n,r)P(n, r).

Repeats allowed? 3-letter codes from 26 letters:

No repeats:26⋅25⋅24=15,60026 \cdot 25 \cdot 24 = 15{,}600   (a permutation)
Repeats allowed:26⋅26⋅26=263=17,57626 \cdot 26 \cdot 26 = 26^3 = 17{,}576   (not a permutation; use the counting principle)
