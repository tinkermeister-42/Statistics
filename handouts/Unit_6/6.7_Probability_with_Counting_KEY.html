6.7  Probability with Counting

Mini Lesson - Unit 6: Probability - DHS Statistics
Teacher Key: answers in red

When every outcome is equally likely, count the outcomes with permutations or combinations.

P(event)=number of ways the event can happentotal number of possible outcomesP(\text{event}) = \dfrac{\text{number of ways the event can happen}}{\text{total number of possible outcomes}}    Count both with the same method.

Example 1: choosing a group. A teacher randomly picks 2 students from 5 girls and 4 boys. What is P(both girls)P(\text{both girls})?

Total ways to pick 2 of 9:9C2=36{}_9C_2 = 36
Ways to pick 2 of the 5 girls:5C2=10{}_5C_2 = 10
Probability:P(both girls)=1036=518≈0.28P(\text{both girls}) = \dfrac{10}{36} = \dfrac{5}{18} \approx 0.28

Example 2: a lottery. Pick 3 different numbers from 1 to 20. Order does not matter. What is P(win)P(\text{win})?

Total: 20C3=1,140{}_{20}C_3 = 1{,}140.   Only 1 of them wins.   P(win)=11,140≈0.0009P(\text{win}) = \dfrac{1}{1{,}140} \approx 0.0009

Example 3: jobs. 10 students are in a club. A president and a vice president are chosen at random. What is the probability that Ana is president and Ben is vice president?

Order matters (different jobs). Total: 10P2=90{}_{10}P_2 = 90.   Only 1 way works.   P=190P = \dfrac{1}{90}

Steps

1.Decide: does order matter? (permutation or combination)
2.Count all the possible outcomes.
3.Count the outcomes you want.
4.Divide.
6.7  Probability with Counting - Practice

Work with a partner. Compare answers when you finish each problem.

1. A committee of 3 is chosen at random from 6 juniors (11th grade) and 4 seniors (12th grade).
  1. How many committees are possible?  10C3=120{}_{10}C_3 = 120
  2. Find P(all seniors)P(\text{all seniors}).
    4C3=4{}_4C_3 = 4.  4120=130\frac{4}{120} = \frac{1}{30}
  3. Find P(all juniors)P(\text{all juniors}).
    6C3=20{}_6C_3 = 20.  20120=16\frac{20}{120} = \frac{1}{6}
2. A lottery: pick 4 different numbers from 1 to 30. Order does not matter. What is the probability of winning with one ticket?
30C4=27,405{}_{30}C_4 = 27{,}405.  P(win)=127,405P(\text{win}) = \frac{1}{27{,}405}
3. 6 runners are in a race. Each order of finish is equally likely. What is the probability that Maya finishes 1st and Leo finishes 2nd?
Order matters. 6P2=30{}_6P_2 = 30.  P=130P = \frac{1}{30}
4. Two cards are dealt from a deck of 52. What is P(both aces)P(\text{both aces})? (There are 4 aces.)
4C2=6{}_4C_2 = 6 and 52C2=1,326{}_{52}C_2 = 1{,}326.  P=61,326=1221P = \frac{6}{1{,}326} = \frac{1}{221}
5. In a taste test, there are 4 drinks from 4 different brands. A person tastes each drink and matches it to a brand. Each brand is used once. If the person just guesses, what is the probability that all 4 are right?
There are 4!=244! = 24 ways to match the brands. Only 1 is right.  P=124P = \frac{1}{24}
6. You grab 2 shirts at random from a drawer of 8 shirts. 3 of the shirts are blue. What is P(both blue)P(\text{both blue})?
3C2=3{}_3C_2 = 3 and 8C2=28{}_8C_2 = 28.  P=328P = \frac{3}{28}
7. A big lottery asks you to pick 5 numbers from 1 to 70 and 1 extra number from 1 to 25.
  1. Use a calculator. How many different tickets are possible?
    70C5⋅25=12,103,014⋅25=302,575,350{}_{70}C_5 \cdot 25 = 12{,}103{,}014 \cdot 25 = 302{,}575{,}350
  2. What is the probability of winning with one ticket? Should you expect to win? Explain.
    1302,575,350\frac{1}{302{,}575{,}350}. No. Winning is extremely unlikely.
