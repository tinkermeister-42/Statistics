One study, every tool. A class flew paper airplanes. Here are 5 flights, in feet.

14, 18, 22, 22, 24

2.1 Find the SD. The SD is about how far the values usually are from the mean.
x‾=100÷5=20\bar{x} = 100 \div 5 = 20 feet
xx1418222224
x−x‾x - \bar{x}−6-6−2-2224

Σ(x−x‾)2=64\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2 = 64    s=Σ(x−x‾)2n−1=644=16=4s = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{64}{4}} = \sqrt{16} = 4 feet
The flights are usually about 4 feet from the mean.
2.2 Choose the measures. Symmetric, no outliers: mean and SD.
Skewed or outliers: median and IQR.
A sixth plane flew 62 feet.
Mean =162÷6=27= 162 \div 6 = 27 feet.   SD ≈17.5\approx 17.5 feet.
Median =22= 22 feet.   Q1 =18= 18,  Q3 =24= 24,  IQR =24−18=6= 24 - 18 = 6 feet.
Upper fence =24+1.5×6=33= 24 + 1.5 \times 6 = 33, so 62 is an outlier. It pulled the mean from 20 up to 27. Use the median and the IQR.
2.3 Where does one value stand? Percentile: the percent of the data below the value.
z-score: how many SDs the value is above or below the mean.		 4 of the 5 flights are shorter than 24 feet. 4÷5=0.804 \div 5 = 0.80. So 24 feet is at the 80th percentile.
All the school's flights: mean μ=20\mu = 20 feet, SD σ=4\sigma = 4 feet.
A 26-foot flight:  z=x−μσ=26−204=1.5z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma} = \dfrac{26 - 20}{4} = 1.5.  It is 1.5 SDs above the mean.
2.4 Use the empirical rule. In a normal distribution, about 68% is within 1 SD of the mean, 95% within 2 SDs, and 99.7% within 3 SDs.
The school's flights have a normal distribution.
68% of the flights are from 16 to 24 feet. 95% are from 12 to 28 feet.
2.5 Use the z-table. The table gives the area below zz. Shorter than 25 feet:  z=25−204=1.25z = \dfrac{25 - 20}{4} = 1.25.  Table: .8944.  89.44%
Longer than 25 feet:  1−.8944=.10561 - .8944 = .1056.  10.56%
2.6 Work in reverse. Find the area inside the table. Read zz. Then x=μ+zσx = \mu + z\sigma. The longest 10% of flights win a prize. Area to the left: 1−.10=.901 - .10 = .90.
Closest area .8997, at z=1.28z = 1.28.  x=20+1.28×4=25.12x = 20 + 1.28 \times 4 = 25.12.  A flight must go about 25.1 feet.

Above or top? Subtract from 1. The z-table always gives the area below zz (to the left).
