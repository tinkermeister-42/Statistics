2.5  Normal Calculations with a Z-Table

Mini Lesson - Unit 2: Analyzing One Variable - DHS Statistics
NameDatePeriod

The empirical rule only works for whole SDs (1, 2, or 3). A z-table works for any z-score.

The z-table gives the area to the left of zz. That is the proportion of the data below zz.

Reading the table: Find z=1.33z = 1.33. Go down to the row 1.3. Go across to the column .03. The area is .9082.

Steps

1.Draw the curve. Shade the part the question asks about.
2.Find each z-score:  z=x−μσz = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}. Round to 2 decimal places.
3.Look up each z-score in the table.
4.Below: use the table.  Above: 1−1 - table.  Between: subtract.

Example: Heights of adult women: mean 64 inches, SD 3 inches.

Shorter than 68 inchesz=68−643=1.33z = \dfrac{68 - 64}{3} = 1.33   table: .9082   90.82%
Taller than 68 inches1−.9082=.09181 - .9082 = .0918   9.18%
From 62 to 68 inchesz=62−643=−0.67z = \dfrac{62 - 64}{3} = -0.67   table: .2514   .9082−.2514=.6568.9082 - .2514 = .6568   65.68%
