6.1 - Venn Diagrams and the Addition Rule

In a class of 30 students, 12 play soccer and 10 play basketball. Pick one student at random. What is the probability the student plays soccer or basketball?

You might add: \(12 + 10 = 22\), so \(\frac{22}{30}\). But 4 students play both. They were counted twice. In this lesson, you learn the rule that fixes this.

probability, complement, union, intersection, addition rule, mutually exclusive

6.1.1 - Probability

Probability is how likely something is, from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). When every outcome is equally likely:

\[ P(A) = \frac{\text{number of ways } A \text{ can happen}}{\text{total number of outcomes}} \]

  • \(P(A)\) is read “the probability of \(A\).”
  • Complement: \(P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)\). Some books write \(P(A^C)\).

Example: A bag has 3 red and 7 blue marbles. Pick one. \(P(\text{red}) = \frac{3}{10}\), and \(P(\text{not red}) = 1 - \frac{3}{10} = \frac{7}{10}\).

6.1.2 - “Or” and “And”

Symbol Read it as Meaning
\(A \cup B\) “\(A\) union \(B\)” or “\(A\) or \(B\)” \(A\) happens, or \(B\) happens, or both
\(A \cap B\) “\(A\) intersect \(B\)” or “\(A\) and \(B\)” \(A\) and \(B\) both happen (the overlap)

If we add \(12 + 10\), the 4 students who play both are counted twice. Subtract them once:

\[ P(S \cup B) = \frac{12}{30} + \frac{10}{30} - \frac{4}{30} = \frac{18}{30} = 0.6 \]

\[ P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) \]

6.1.3 - Mutually Exclusive Events

Mutually exclusive events cannot both happen. Then \(P(A \cap B) = 0\), so

\[ P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) \]

Example: Roll a number cube. Rolling a 1 and rolling a 2 cannot both happen. \(P(1 \cup 2) = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{3}\).

A standard deck has 52 cards: 4 suits (hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades), with 13 cards in each suit (A, 2 to 10, J, Q, K).

They are not mutually exclusive: the queen of hearts and the queen of diamonds are both.

\[ \frac{26}{52} + \frac{4}{52} - \frac{2}{52} = \frac{28}{52} = \frac{7}{13} \]

The Addition Rule

  1. In a class of 40 students, 18 take Spanish (\(S\)), 15 take art (\(A\)), and 6 take both.

    1. Draw and fill in a Venn diagram.

    2. Find \(P(S \cup A)\).

    3. Find \(P(S \cap A)\).

    4. Find the probability that a student takes neither.

  2. \(P(A) = 0.5\), \(P(B) = 0.4\), and \(P(A \cap B) = 0.2\). Find \(P(A \cup B)\).

  3. Draw one card from a deck. Are the events mutually exclusive? Find the probability.

    1. a heart or a spade

    2. a heart or a king

  4. Pick one student from this table at random.

    Bus Car Walk Total
    9th grade 18 12 10 40
    10th grade 14 20 6 40
    Total 32 32 16 80

    1. \(P(\text{9th grade} \cap \text{car})\)

    2. \(P(\text{10th grade} \cup \text{walk})\)

  5. A student says, “\(P(A) = 0.6\) and \(P(B) = 0.5\), so \(P(A \cup B) = 1.1\).” What did the student do wrong?
