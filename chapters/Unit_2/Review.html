Unit 2 Review

A class flew paper airplanes. Here are 5 flights, in feet:

14, 18, 22, 22, 24

Every tool from Unit 2 tells you something about these flights. The standard deviation shows their spread. A percentile and a z-score tell where one flight stands. The normal curve and the z-table find percents for all the flights in the school.

This review uses all of them. It has every kind of problem on the Unit 2 quiz, with more of each.

standard deviation, variance, center, spread, outlier, IQR, percentile, z-score, mu, sigma, normal distribution, empirical rule, z-table

Printable review - notes and practice problems to print and write on.

Z-table - the standard normal table, two pages. You need it for 2.5 and 2.6.

2.1 - Find the SD

The standard deviation (SD) is about how far the values usually are from the mean.

\[ \bar{x} = 100 \div 5 = 20 \text{ feet} \]

\(x\) \(x - \bar{x}\) \((x - \bar{x})^2\)
14 \(-6\) 36
18 \(-2\) 4
22 2 4
22 2 4
24 4 16
\(\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2\) 64

\[ s = \sqrt{\frac{\Sigma(x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}} = \sqrt{\frac{64}{4}} = \sqrt{16} = 4 \text{ feet} \]

The flights are usually about 4 feet from the mean.

2.2 - Choose the Measures

Shape of the data Center Spread
Symmetric, no outliers mean standard deviation
Skewed, or has outliers median IQR

A sixth plane flew 62 feet.

  • Mean \(= 162 \div 6 = 27\) feet. SD \(\approx 17.5\) feet.
  • Median \(= 22\) feet. Q1 \(= 18\), Q3 \(= 24\), IQR \(= 24 - 18 = 6\) feet.
  • Upper fence \(= 24 + 1.5 \times 6 = 33\), so 62 is an outlier.

One flight pulled the mean from 20 up to 27. Use the median and the IQR.

2.3 - Where One Value Stands

  • The percentile of a value is the percent of the data below it.
  • A z-score tells how many SDs a value is above or below the mean: \(z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}\).
  • \(\mu\) (mu) is the mean. \(\sigma\) (small sigma) is the SD.

4 of the 5 flights are shorter than 24 feet. \(4 \div 5 = 0.80\). So 24 feet is at the 80th percentile.

All the school’s flights have a mean \(\mu = 20\) feet and an SD \(\sigma = 4\) feet. A flight is 26 feet long:

\[ z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} = \frac{26 - 20}{4} = 1.5 \]

It is 1.5 SDs above the mean.

2.4 - Use the Empirical Rule

The school’s flights have a normal distribution.

  • About 68% of the data is within 1 SD of the mean.
  • About 95% of the data is within 2 SDs of the mean.
  • About 99.7% of the data is within 3 SDs of the mean.

68% of the flights are from 16 to 24 feet. 95% are from 12 to 28 feet.

2.5 - Use the Z-Table

The z-table gives the area below \(z\).

Question Work Answer
Shorter than 25 feet \(z = \frac{25 - 20}{4} = 1.25\), table: .8944 89.44%
Longer than 25 feet \(1 - .8944 = .1056\) 10.56%

The z-table always gives the area below \(z\) (to the left).

2.6 - Work in Reverse

The longest 10% of flights win a prize. How far must a flight go?

Step Work
1. Find the area to the left. \(1 - .10 = .90\)
2. Find the closest area inside the table. .8997, at \(z = 1.28\)
3. Find the value: \(x = \mu + z\sigma\) \(20 + 1.28 \times 4 = 25.12\)

A flight must go about 25.1 feet.

Test-Style Questions

A test asks the same questions with more words. Read slowly. Choose the best answer.

  1. Each data set below has a mean of 50. Which data set has the greatest standard deviation?

    A. 48, 49, 50, 51, 52
    B. 30, 40, 50, 60, 70
    C. 45, 50, 50, 50, 55
    D. 50, 50, 50, 50, 50

  2. A restaurant recorded the number of minutes that each of 50 customers waited for a table. The distribution of the wait times is strongly skewed to the right and has several outliers. Which measures of center and spread are most appropriate to describe the wait times?

    A. the median and the interquartile range
    B. the mean and the standard deviation
    C. the mean and the interquartile range
    D. the median and the standard deviation

  3. On a science test, the class mean was 72 points with a standard deviation of 6 points. On a history test, the class mean was 80 points with a standard deviation of 5 points. Mia scored 84 points on the science test and 88 points on the history test. Which statement is true?

    A. Mia did better on the history test compared to her class, because 88 is greater than 84.
    B. Mia did equally well on both tests compared to her class, because both scores are above the class mean.
    C. Mia’s history score was 1.6 points above the class mean.
    D. Mia did better on the science test compared to her class, because her z-score was greater on that test.

  4. The lifetimes of a type of light bulb are approximately normally distributed with a mean of 900 hours and a standard deviation of 50 hours. According to the empirical rule, approximately what percent of the light bulbs last between 800 hours and 950 hours?

    A. 47.5%
    B. 68%
    C. 81.5%
    D. 95%

  5. The scores on a math contest are approximately normally distributed with a mean of 60 points and a standard deviation of 8 points. Use the z-table. What proportion of the scores are greater than 74 points?

    A. .0401
    B. .4599
    C. .5401
    D. .9599

  6. The weights of watermelons at a farm are approximately normally distributed with a mean of 20 pounds and a standard deviation of 2.5 pounds. The heaviest 3% of the watermelons win a blue ribbon. Use the z-table. Which weight is closest to the lightest watermelon that wins a blue ribbon?

    A. 15.3 pounds
    B. 22.5 pounds
    C. 24.7 pounds
    D. 25.0 pounds
